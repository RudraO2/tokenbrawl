# Story 4.8 — A BYOK Match that paces itself and survives a rate limit

As a visitor running my own fight, I want the runner to stay inside my quota on purpose and treat a pause as a pause, so that thirty successful calls are not thrown away because the thirty-first arrived a second too early.

## Why this exists

A Match is up to two provider calls per Decision Point across roughly thirty
Decision Points — **~60 calls worst case**. Measured free-tier limits
(`docs/reports/byok-provider-limits.md`, read it first):

- **Groq**: 30 RPM, but **6–12K TPM**. At roughly a thousand tokens a call, TPM
  allows about **6–12 calls a minute** — a Match takes **5–10 minutes** and RPM
  is never the constraint.
- **Cerebras**: **5 RPM**. A Match takes a minimum of **twelve minutes**.
- **Google**: 5–30 RPM depending on model, and several models cap at **20
  requests a day**, which cannot cover one Match at all.

So a BYOK Match does not *occasionally* hit a limit. It hits one **every time**,
by arithmetic. Story 4.6 turns that into a failed Match with nothing kept —
deliberately, because the alternative it was avoiding is worse: the adapters
*resolve* a 429 and let the Decision Point become a Parse Failure, publishing a
Match the visitor's quota never actually played. Discarding was right with the
time available. It is not the right end state.

**The distinction everything here turns on, and it must not be blurred.** INV-1
forbids re-asking a model *after it has answered* — that is what would let a
Match depend on how many attempts a Deployment needed. A 429 produced no answer
at all. Waiting for a provider to accept the *first* call is not a retry of a
decision; it is the decision still being made. Story 3.2's adapter already backs
off once on exactly this reasoning.

## The pacing signal already exists

Groq returns these on **every** response, not only on a 429:

| Header | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `x-ratelimit-remaining-tokens` | TPM remaining |
| `x-ratelimit-reset-tokens` | when TPM refills, e.g. `7.66s` |
| `x-ratelimit-remaining-requests` | RPD remaining |
| `x-ratelimit-reset-requests` | when RPD refills |
| `retry-after` | seconds; **only** on a 429 |

That is enough to slow down *before* hitting a wall rather than absorbing one and
recovering. Prefer proactive pacing; keep reactive waiting as the floor for
providers that report nothing.

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** a provider that reports remaining quota, **When** the runner is close to exhausting it, **Then** it paces the next call to stay inside the limit rather than waiting to be refused.

**Given** a rate-limited call mid-Match, **When** the provider supplies a retry interval, **Then** the runner waits and repeats **that same call**, and the Match continues from the Decision Point it was at with nothing re-decided.

**Given** a Match that paused or paced, **When** its Command Log is inspected, **Then** it is byte-identical to the log the same Match would produce with no pause at all — pacing leaves no trace, because nothing about it may affect outcome (INV-1, INV-2).

**Given** a successful call, **When** anything at all happens afterwards, **Then** that call is never issued a second time.

**Given** a visitor waiting through a pause, **When** the panel reports it, **Then** it reports a state and a count of completed calls, and never a duration, an estimate, a countdown or a per-model wait (INV-3).

**Given** a key that is invalid, revoked, or out of *daily* quota, **When** it fails, **Then** the runner stops immediately rather than waiting — a wait that cannot succeed is worse than a failure.

**Given** a Match that has waited more than a bounded number of times, **When** the bound is reached, **Then** the Match is abandoned with an attributed message and no partially-recorded Command Log.

**Given** a model whose daily cap cannot cover a whole Match, **When** it is selected, **Then** the visitor is told before the first call, not after the thirtieth.

**Given** `packages/providers`, **When** this story lands, **Then** `git diff` shows no change to any adapter's rate-limit path: tournament behaviour is untouched.

## Scope

`apps/web/src/byok/`. The waiting and the pacing belong to the BYOK runner, not
to the adapters — `packages/providers` is stateless per call by contract (AD-9)
and must stay that way. Reading rate-limit headers into a typed signal is
already `rate-limit.ts`'s job and it may be extended there; deciding what to do
about the signal is the runner's.

## Invariants at risk

- **INV-1** — the boundary above. Re-issuing a call that produced no answer is
  permitted; re-asking after an answer is not, ever. A test must pin that a
  successful call is never issued twice, and it must be a test that fails if the
  guard is removed.
- **INV-3** — nothing the visitor sees may encode how long a model takes to
  think. A count of completed calls is fine. A countdown, an ETA, a spinner
  whose behaviour varies with the provider, or "waiting 47s for Gemini" is not.
  The pacing *delay itself* is derived from quota headers rather than from model
  latency, which is what keeps it clean — but it must never be displayed as a
  duration.
- **INV-2** — a paced Match must be exactly as deterministic as an uninterrupted
  one. Pacing must not perturb ordering, seeding, simultaneity, or the Token
  Bank. The byte-identical-log test is the proof.

## Test plan

A transport that 429s at call N then succeeds produces a log identical to one
that never 429s. A transport reporting dwindling `x-ratelimit-remaining-tokens`
causes pacing without any 429 occurring. A successful call is never repeated. A
401 stops immediately without waiting. The wait bound is enforced and its
exhaustion abandons the Match with an attributed message and no log. No timing
term appears anywhere on the status path. Adapter behaviour is unchanged.

Mutation-check the INV-1 boundary specifically: make the runner re-issue a call
that *did* answer, and confirm a test goes red.
