# Story 4.8 — A BYOK Match that survives a rate limit

As a visitor running my own fight, I want a quota pause to be a pause rather than a total loss, so that thirty successful calls are not thrown away because the thirty-first arrived a second too early.

## Why this exists

A full Match is up to two provider calls per Decision Point across roughly
thirty Decision Points. Free tiers are 30 RPM (Groq, Cerebras) and 5–15 RPM
(Google AI Studio). A visitor on Google is therefore rate-limited part-way
through by arithmetic, not by bad luck.

Story 4.6 turns that into a failed Match with nothing kept — deliberately, because
the alternative it was avoiding is worse: `createGroqClient` *resolves* a 429 and
lets the Decision Point become a Parse Failure, which would publish a Match the
visitor's quota never actually played. Discarding was the correct call with the
time available. It is not the right end state.

**The distinction this story turns on, and it must not be blurred:** INV-1
forbids re-asking a model *after it has answered* — that is what would let a
Match depend on how many attempts a Deployment needed. A 429 produced no answer
at all. Waiting for the provider to accept the *first* call is not a retry of a
decision; it is the decision still being made. Story 3.2's adapter already backs
off once on exactly this reasoning.

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** a rate-limited call mid-Match, **When** the provider supplies a retry interval, **Then** the runner waits and repeats **that same call**, and the Match continues from the Decision Point it was at with nothing re-decided.

**Given** a Match that resumed after a pause, **When** its Command Log is inspected, **Then** it is byte-identical to the log the same Match would have produced with no pause — a pause leaves no trace, because nothing about it may affect outcome (INV-1).

**Given** a visitor waiting through a pause, **When** the panel reports it, **Then** it reports a state and a count of calls, never a duration, an estimate or a countdown (INV-3).

**Given** a key that is invalid, revoked or out of daily quota, **When** it fails, **Then** the runner stops — a wait that cannot succeed is worse than a failure, and there must be a bounded number of waits after which the Match is abandoned and said so.

**Given** an abandoned Match, **When** it is abandoned, **Then** no partially-recorded Command Log is produced, exactly as in 4.6.

**Given** a model name the provider rejects as unknown or retired, **When** it fails, **Then** the visitor is told that specific thing rather than "the provider returned an error".

## Scope

`apps/web/src/byok/`. The waiting belongs to the BYOK runner, not to the
adapters: `packages/providers` is stateless per call by contract (AD-9) and must
stay that way. Tournament behaviour must not change — verify with `git diff` that
no adapter's rate-limit path was touched.

## Invariants at risk

- **INV-1** — the boundary above. Re-issuing a call that produced no answer is
  permitted; re-asking after an answer is not, ever. A test must pin that a
  successful call is never issued twice.
- **INV-3** — nothing the visitor sees during a wait may encode how long a model
  takes to think. A count of completed calls is fine; a spinner whose behaviour
  varies, a countdown, or an ETA is not.
- **INV-2** — the resumed Match must be as deterministic as an uninterrupted one.
  A pause must not perturb ordering, seeding, or the Token Bank.

## Test plan

A transport that 429s at call N then succeeds produces a log identical to one
that never 429s. A successful call is never repeated. A bounded wait count is
enforced and its exhaustion abandons the Match with an attributed message. A
401 stops immediately without waiting. No timing term appears anywhere on the
status path. Tournament adapter behaviour is unchanged.

## Exit gate

`npm test` green **AND** `bash scripts/audit-invariants.sh` exiting zero.
