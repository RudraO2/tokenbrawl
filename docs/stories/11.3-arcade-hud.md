# Story 11.3 — The HUD joins the game

As a visitor, I want the health bars and Super Gauge to look like an arcade
fighter's HUD, so that the instrument panel does not sit at odds with the fight
underneath it.

## Reference material

**Read `docs/DEV-REFERENCE.md` first** for the reference project's location
(`<REF>` below) and the four mechanisms that may never be copied.

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Skewed bar path | `<REF>/game_source/js/screens.js:2283` — `hudParaPath` |
| Rounded path helper | `screens.js:2291` — `hudRoundRect` |
| Vertical gradient helper | `screens.js:2302` — `hudVGrad` |
| Percentage fill, used for both the live bar and the lag ghost | `screens.js:2339` — `hudFillPct` |
| Health bar, lag ghost, colour tiers | `screens.js:2450-2458` — green above 50%, gold above 25%, red below |
| Super meter: 4 skewed segments, whole bar gold and pulsing at 100 | `screens.js:2460-2472` |
| Reference screenshot to compare against | `<REF>/shots/04_local_match.png` |

**One thing in that HUD needs translating rather than copying.** The full-meter
pulse is `0.6 + 0.4 * Math.abs(Math.sin(state.t / 8))`. `state.t` is a rendered-
frame counter, so a pulse derived from **this project's frame index** is
legitimate and is not a wall-clock read. What is *not* legitimate is a pulse
driven by anything else, or one that survives `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## The ruling this story implements

The HUD is drawn on the **canvas**, not in HTML, so "UI versus game" was never a
clean line for it. The owner ruled on 2026-08-07 that **the canvas HUD counts as
game**: glow, gradient fills, bevels, chunky arcade type, an aura-tinted gauge.

Page chrome — the panels around the canvas, the buttons, the leaderboard, the
reasoning cards — **stays flat**. That boundary is the whole point of Story
11.1 and this story must not blur it.

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** the health bars, **When** they are drawn, **Then** they read as arcade
bars: a bevelled frame, a gradient fill, and a lag/chip layer behind the current
value so a big hit is visible as a receding second bar rather than only as a
number changing.

**Given** the Super Gauge, **When** it fills, **Then** it reads as charging —
and **When** it is full, **Then** the armed state is unmistakable at the size it
actually renders, not zoomed in.

**Given** the tick counter and callouts, **When** they are drawn, **Then** they
use the arcade type treatment rather than the flat body face.

**Given** the full gauge, **When** it pulses, **Then** the pulse is a pure
function of the **frame index** — the same index the rest of the juice layer
runs on — so the same frame drawn twice is identical and a scrub reproduces it.
A pulse driven by anything else is a wall clock wearing a costume (INV-1,
INV-3).

**Given** `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, **When** the HUD is drawn, **Then**
the pulse and every other self-animating element is switched off, and the HUD is
otherwise unchanged — same layout, same colours, same information.

**Given** the page chrome, **When** it is inspected, **Then** nothing about it
changed: the panels, buttons and leaderboard keep every flat-surface rule.

**Given** any Match, **When** its Final-State Hash is compared before and after,
**Then** it is unchanged (AD-15). The HUD reads state; it never writes it.

## Scope

`apps/web/src/render/renderer.ts`, `apps/web/src/render/theme.ts`. No stylesheet
change — if this story appears to need one, the boundary has been crossed.

## Frozen contracts touched

None.

## Invariants at risk

- **AD-15** — presentation only.
- **INV-3** — nothing in the HUD may animate on a clock or encode elapsed time.
  A lag bar is driven by *frame index*, like every other effect in this repo.

## Test plan

- The HUD draws a lag layer distinct from the current-health layer.
- The armed gauge and the charging gauge produce different call sequences.
- The same frame index drawn twice yields the same call sequence, pulse included.
- Reduced motion produces a still HUD with the same layout and information.
- Final-State Hash unchanged.
- The stylesheet discipline sweep still passes untouched.

## Visual gate — the finding (Story 10.1)

Chrome DevTools MCP against `http://localhost:5173`, all three surfaces,
captured at the size they actually render and again at 1:1. Console filtered to
`error` and `warn`: **clean** — the single warning present was
`getImageData ... willReadFrequently`, raised by the gate's own pixel probes and
not by the app. No asset loader reported a failed decode.

**Replay player (`#app`).** The HUD reads as arcade: skewed parallelogram bars
with a lit bevel and an outlined frame, a real four-band gradient across the
health fill, and gold armed gauges. Pixel probe over the HUD band confirms every
structural colour is actually painted rather than merely called —
`hudPlate` 6177px, `hudFrame` 5440px, `hudBevel` 884px, `hudGhost` 32px on a
frame where a hit was landing, and all four `hpHigh` band stops present. The
`HP … MTR …` readouts stay legible on the mono face at the rendered size.

**Spectate (`#spectate`).** Same HUD, and the surface that showed the states the
replay player did not: p1 at HP 16 on the red tier with a **charging** cyan
gauge (one of four segments lit), p2 at HP 56 on the green tier with an armed
gold one. The mirroring is correct and is the thing worth having looked at —
p1's segments light from the left edge, p2's from the right, so the pair reads
as two fighters facing each other rather than as one panel drawn twice.

**Arcade (`#arcade`).** Play-vs-CPU takes over the same `.tb-canvas` stage and
draws through the same `drawFrame`, so it is not an independent regression
surface. Captured with a Match running (status "Fighting"): plate, frame, bevel
and all four `hpHigh` stops present.

### What the gate caught that the suite could not

**One defect, found before commit.** The bevel was drawn on the bar's own top
row and then painted over *completely* by the frame rule, which is drawn last
and is two pixels deep. Every call-log assertion passed — the bevel call was
issued, with correct arguments — and it was `hero/raster.ts`, where pixels are
actually composited, that failed. The bevel now sits at row `FRAME_THICKNESS`,
inside the frame. `hud.test.ts` gained a geometry assertion so the next
equivalent slip does not need the hero to find it.

**One defect, found only by looking.** `ULTIMATE READY` was drawn across the
armed gauge while the armed gauge was still four gapped segments, so the gaps
cut through the letters. At 1:1 it was legible; at the size the canvas actually
renders — scaled to its column, ~71% — it degraded to mush, which fails AC2's
"unmistakable at the size it actually renders, not zoomed in". The fix was to
follow the reference one step further than the first pass had: it segments the
meter while charging and turns **the whole bar** gold at full
(`screens.js:2460-2472`). Armed is now one solid bar. That gives the callout an
unbroken ground *and* makes charging and armed differ in **shape** rather than
only in colour, which is a distinction that survives being glanced at.
Re-captured after the change: the callout reads cleanly on both fighters.

### Compared against the reference

`<REF>/shots/04_local_match.png`. Bar geometry, skew direction, tier colours and
the segmented-charging/solid-armed meter all match. Two deliberate differences,
both recorded in `render/arena-palette.ts`: the plate is drawn **opaque** rather
than at the reference's 85%, because `hero/raster.ts` stores palette indices and
cannot composite; and the full-meter pulse is three enumerated gold ramps on a
`frame.index` triangle rather than the reference's `globalAlpha` sine, which is
a wall clock in the reference and generates unbounded colours a palette cannot
hold.
