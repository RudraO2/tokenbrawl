# Story 10.3 — The Super Gauge reads as a weapon being charged

As a visitor, I want the bar under the health bar to visibly charge and then arm, so that I can see an Ultimate coming before it lands.

## Why this story exists

The meter is already drawn — `renderer.ts` paints it as a 10px bar under the health bar, filled in `theme.ink`. It is a hairline, it looks like a rule rather than a resource, and at full it looks exactly as it does at 99%. A visitor has no way to know an Ultimate is available, which is the entire tension the Action is supposed to create.

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** the meter bar in `renderer.ts`, **When** meter is below full, **Then** it fills proportionally and is legibly a *gauge* — sized so a viewer reads it as a resource being accumulated, not as a divider.

**Given** meter at full, **When** the bar is drawn, **Then** it enters an armed state that is unmistakable at a glance: the bar reads `ULTIMATE READY` in ground ink over a filled bar, in the same warn-as-fill language `drawTokenBank` already uses for `REFLEX`. Reusing that pattern is deliberate — the page already teaches a viewer that an inverted, filled bar carrying a word means *a threshold has been crossed*.

**Given** the armed state, **When** it is animated at all, **Then** any pulse is driven by the frame counter the juice layer already uses (`frame.index`), never by a clock or a `Date` (INV-1, INV-3). A replay seeked to the same frame twice must draw the identical gauge.

**Given** `docs/DESIGN.md`, **When** the gauge is styled, **Then** it obeys the house style — flat fills, square corners, hard offset shadow, no gradient, no glow, no translucency. `fillRect` cannot produce a rounded corner, so most of this holds by construction; the rule still gets checked rather than assumed.

**Given** a Baseline Bot with no Token Bank, **When** the gauge is drawn, **Then** the existing bank/meter distinction is preserved — this story changes the Super Meter's presentation only, and must not disturb Story 4.4's Token Bank row beneath it.

**Given** all three rendering surfaces, **When** the gauge is checked, **Then** the replay player, Arcade and Spectate each show it (Story 10.1).

**Given** any Match, **When** its Final-State Hash is compared before and after this story, **Then** it is unchanged. This story is presentation only.

## Completion note

Shipped 2026-08-06. Presentation only: `apps/web/src/render/renderer.ts` and its test. `theme.ts` was in scope but needed nothing — the armed state is built from colours the palette already had, so no token moved and `style-discipline.test.ts`'s "every hex lives in tokens.css or theme.ts" sweep had nothing new to see.

**What changed.** Three things, all inside `renderer.ts`.

1. `METER_HEIGHT` 10 → 18. The 10px hairline was the defect: drawn the width of the health bar in `theme.ink` — the colour every *border* and every *rule* on the canvas uses — it read as a divider between the health bar and the readout beneath it. Eighteen is deliberately short of the health bar's twenty, so the gauge is legibly a second resource rather than a second health bar, and it leaves room for the armed word at the mono face's real size. `METER_TOP` was extracted at the same time; `HUD_LABEL_BASELINE` and `BANK_TOP` still derive from it, so Story 4.4's Token Bank row moved down by the same 8px it grew and its relationship to everything above is unchanged.
2. A new `drawSuperGauge`, sitting beside `drawTokenBank` because it borrows its language. Below threshold it is an ordinary proportional bar. At threshold it inverts to a solid `--tb-warn` block carrying `ULTIMATE READY` in ground ink — the same warn-as-fill pattern, on purpose rather than by coincidence: the page has already taught a viewer that an inverted filled bar with a word in it means a threshold has been crossed, and a second visual language for one idea would have to teach it twice. Ground ink on the warn fill, never the reverse, for the measured reason `docs/DESIGN.md` records (`--tb-warn` on `--tb-bg` is 4.26:1 and misses the 4.5:1 floor).
3. A frame-counted pulse. The armed bar's **border** alternates between `--tb-ink` and `--tb-accent` every `ARMED_PULSE_FRAMES` (12) film frames. A flat border swap is the only pulse the house style leaves available — a glow, a fade or a scale each need something `docs/DESIGN.md` bans outright, and `style-discipline.test.ts` enforces the translucency half of that ban directly. The phase is `Math.floor(frame.index / 12) % 2`, so it reads no clock and a seek to frame 90 draws the same half of the blink as a play-through that reaches frame 90.

**On the threshold.** The gauge arms on `meter >= specialMeterCost`, not on `meter >= maxMeter`. ULTIMATE READY is a claim about the Action being *legal*, and `specialMeterCost` is the number `legalActionsFor` actually gates on — reading the same field is what stops the HUD asserting something the simulation would refuse. Story 10.2 set the two equal, so under the shipped config this is exactly "at full and at no other value", which is what the AC asks for; a later story that lowers the cost inherits a gauge that still tells the truth instead of one that quietly goes stale.

**Hash neutrality.** No `packages/` file was touched and no Command Log changed — `git status` across the whole change is `renderer.ts`, `renderer.test.ts` and one regenerated image. The hero artefact test caught the one real consequence: `docs/hero/hero.gif` is a *render* artefact, so a renderer change necessarily changes its bytes. Regenerated through its own documented flag (`apps/web/scripts/build-hero.mts`), never hand-edited. `hero.command-log.json` is byte-identical, and both `is exactly what the generator produces today` and `replays to the hash it records` pass unchanged — which is the Final-State Hash claim, asserted rather than argued.

**One existing test was widened.** `uses only theme colours -- no hex is invented at draw time` drew its sweep frame at `meter: [80, 15]`, which misses the armed branch entirely. It now draws at `maxMeter`, so the one path that sets a warn fill and the one path that sets an accent stroke are both inside the sweep. That is the lesson the test's own comment already recorded about Story 4.4's two colour paths; this story would have re-learned it otherwise.

`npm test` green (776 + 262 across the workspaces). `bash scripts/audit-invariants.sh` exits zero.

## Visual findings (Story 10.1 gate)

All three surfaces captured in Chrome at 1440×900, in the empty, charging and armed states. Console clean of application errors at the end.

- **Replay player (`#app`)** — correct, and the story's premise is visibly fixed. Empty (`MTR 0`) is now a bordered trough that reads as a container awaiting fill rather than as a rule. Charging is discriminable at a glance: at DP 7 the two gauges read `MTR 30` and `MTR 24` and are plainly different lengths, where before this story a 10px hairline at 18 and one at 100 were the same picture. Armed is unmistakable — solid red bar, `ULTIMATE READY` in ground ink. Backdrop present, both fighters distinct and at a sane scale with feet on the floor, `HASH VERIFIED`.
- **The 100-vs-96 pair, which is the actual test of this story.** Seeking the demo Match to clock 236 puts `MTR 100` beside `MTR 96` — the closest two meters ever get without one being armed. The armed gauge is a red block with a word in it; the 96 is a near-full ink bar with a visible sliver of unfilled trough at its right end. No ambiguity at normal viewing size.
- **The pulse was verified by pixel probe, not by eye.** Sampling the gauge band at two frames one half-period apart: at film 216 and film 228 the warn fill is 4027px in *both*, and exactly 2616px flip from `#c8ff00` to `#f5f5f0` — the border, and nothing else. Same-frame-same-picture confirmed by the identical counts; the flip confirmed by the one colour that moved. No colour outside the five-token palette appeared in either sample.
- **Arcade (`#arcade`)** — correct. Arcade plays headlessly and `startup.ts` re-mounts the player with the finished log, so its rendering surface *is* the `#app` canvas; verified by playing a real Match through the on-screen buttons rather than by asserting the shared code path. The resulting replay is labelled `P1:HUMAN` vs `P2:BOT:RANDOM` and badged `ARCADE · NOT RATED`, and at DP 30 it shows an armed `MTR 100` gauge beside a charging `MTR 60` one.
- **Spectate (`#spectate`)** — correct. Charging captured on spectate-03 at `MTR 12` vs `MTR 24`, clearly different fills. Armed captured on spectate-02 at `MTR 100` vs `MTR 96`, with the accent half of the pulse on the border. The stream advances faster than a screenshot round-trip, so the armed frame was captured by copying the live canvas's own pixels into a pinned still at the moment of detection — a real capture of the rendered surface, not a re-render. All six entries load and no hash-verification skips appeared in the console, so Story 10.2's regeneration of the spectate manifest is still good.

**Three observations, none of them blocking, none fixed here:**

1. *An armed p2 at full health stacks two warn blocks.* p2's health bar is `--tb-warn` and so is the armed gauge, so a p2 that is both untouched and armed shows two red bars separated by an 8px gap. It stays legible — the ground-ink word and the 4px ink border both carry — and it resolves the instant p2 takes any damage, since the health bar then shortens and the gauge does not. Accepted rather than worked around: the alternative is a third loud colour, which `docs/DESIGN.md` calls scope creep by name. Worth a second look in **10.4**, which may want the cinematic to own the arming moment anyway.
2. *A human can barely charge the bar in Arcade, and that is a 10.6 problem.* Three Matches were played by hand to find an armed frame. Spamming `attack` reached 24% of a bar; spamming `block` at range reached 1%; only alternating `advance`/`attack` — re-establishing range after each committal window — reached a full bar, and not until DP 30 of 40. With 10.2 having made the Ultimate cost the whole bar, **Story 10.6's `L` key may spend most of an Arcade Match with nothing to fire.** That is a balance observation about 10.2's economy meeting a human's input rate, not a defect in this story, but 10.6 should not assume the gauge will be armed when it goes looking for it.
3. *10.2's edge-clipping defect reproduced* in the Arcade capture — both fighters walked to the right wall and lost part of their bodies off-canvas. Left alone, as instructed; it remains logged in 10.2.
