# Story 10.4 — The Ultimate is a cinematic, not a frame

As a visitor, I want the Ultimate to stop the fight and command attention, so that it lands like an arcade super rather than a fast poke.

## The decision that shapes this story

The reference freezes **both fighters for 90 ticks inside its simulation** (`CINEMATIC_FREEZE = 90` in its `core.js`). Porting that literally would change Tick counts, Match length, the Decision Point budget, and every Final-State Hash in this repository — turning a presentation flourish into a balance change and invalidating every committed Command Log.

**So the freeze is held by the renderer, over playback frames, and the simulation is untouched.** AD-15 already requires the juice layer to be hash-neutral, and Story 9.5's frame-counted juice layer already exists to hang this on. A Match hashes identically with the cinematic present or removed, and that is asserted rather than assumed.

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** an Ultimate entering its active phase, **When** playback reaches it, **Then** the renderer holds a freeze for a fixed number of **playback frames**, during which the fight does not advance on screen.

**Given** that freeze, **When** the simulation is examined, **Then** it is completely unaffected: Tick count, Match length, Decision Point budget and Final-State Hash are byte-identical whether the cinematic layer is enabled or disabled. A test proves this by hashing the same Match both ways and asserting equality.

**Given** the freeze, **When** its duration is expressed, **Then** it is counted in frames, never in milliseconds and never from a clock (INV-1, INV-3).

**Given** the timeline scrubber from Story 4.5, **When** a visitor seeks across an Ultimate, **Then** playback remains seek-safe: the cinematic is a pure function of the frame index, so scrubbing backwards and forwards over it produces identical output rather than a stuck or double-triggered effect.

**Given** the Ultimate connecting, **When** it is drawn, **Then** it carries a flash, a screen shake and an impact mark through the **existing** frame-counted juice layer — extended, never reimplemented alongside it.

**Given** the four roster packs, **When** the Ultimate plays, **Then** it uses the existing `special-startup` / `special-active` / `special-recovery` clips. All four packs already ship distinct art for these three phases — verified by content hash, they are not copies of the attack clips — so no new art is required by this story.

**Given** a Baseline Bot Match with no Ultimate thrown, **When** it plays back, **Then** nothing about it changes.

## Standing rule — translate, do not reimplement

This story carries the same risk Story 9.5 flagged and it is repeated here because it is the likeliest way this story goes wrong. The reference's VFX are written against a 60fps real-time loop with its own particle pool and its own RNG. **Do not port that pool.** Express the same *look* in this project's frame-counted, deterministic, hash-neutral juice layer. A particle system that reads a clock or draws from an ungoverned RNG breaks INV-1 and AD-15 at once, and would do so while still producing a plausible-looking screenshot.

### The freeze is the hitstop mechanism, not a second one

`buildJuiceTrack` already re-presented a film frame for N extra clock frames. The cinematic is that same hold, longer, keyed on a different trigger — the film frame the Ultimate's **active phase** opens on. One `Math.max` decides the hold when a hit and an Ultimate land on the same film frame, exactly as two simultaneous hits already resolve to the longer hitstop rather than to their sum.

Two consequences fall out for free, and both are asserted rather than argued:

- **The simulation is untouched.** Same film, same Tick counts, same Decision Point budget, same Final-State Hash. The freeze exists on the *clock* axis, which the film has no knowledge of.
- **`player/clock.ts` needed no change.** It is already handed `track.frameCount`, which is the film's length plus every hold, so a 90-frame cinematic lengthens the transport and the scrub's `max` automatically. The story listed `apps/web/src/player/` in scope; the correct outcome there was to change nothing, because Story 9.5 had already put the mapping in the right place.

`filmIndexAt` still visits every film frame exactly once as a live frame — `cinematic-neutrality.test.ts` asserts that on a Match that actually contains an Ultimate, so the fight still plays in full and only the number of callbacks changes.

### Where the trigger comes from, and why `renderer.ts` grew two exports

`specialWindow` is `10` startup / `5` active / `45` recovery Ticks and a Decision Point is `30`, so the Ultimate's active phase opens and closes **strictly between two samples of the simulation**. Reading `frame.from.committedAction` would put the cinematic on a fighter already deep in recovery, or on no frame at all — the identical trap `renderer.ts`'s `liveWindow` was written for during Story 4.1, when a census found `attack-active` played on zero of 360 frames.

So `liveWindow` and a new `ticksIntoDecision` are now exported from `renderer.ts` and *asked* by `juice.ts` rather than reproduced in it. Under the shipped frame data that reconstruction puts the Ultimate on exactly one film frame per throw (offset 5 of the committing Decision Point, Tick 12 of the window). `deriveCinematicEvents` still emits only the first frame of each active *run*, so a later story that lengthens `specialWindow.active` gets one cinematic instead of several.

`HUD_BOTTOM` is the third new export, derived from `BANK_TOP + BANK_HEIGHT` rather than written down, so the banner follows the HUD if a later story grows the gauge again the way 10.3 did.

### Hash neutrality — the story's central risk

`cinematic-neutrality.test.ts` is the assertion, and it is deliberately **not** run against the demo Match. Neither Baseline Bot ever submits `special` (`packages/env-fighter/src/bots.ts` contains no such string), so every case would have passed on it with the cinematic code unreached. The one committed Command Log in this repository that contains an Ultimate is `spectate-03.command-log.json` — the random bot spends a full bar at Tick 870 and connects for 22 — and that is what the hash claim is made against.

The load-bearing case runs the full juiced paint over all 630 clock frames and only *then* rebuilds the film from the same log with a fresh environment: a drawing path that had written back into a `FighterState` would show up there and nowhere else. `cinematic.freezeFrames: 0` is a real off switch (no hold, no record, no call), so "enabled vs disabled" is a comparison with teeth: the hashes are identical, the frame counts differ by exactly 90, and the call sequences differ.

The demo Match still appears, as the other half of the promise — a Baseline Bot Match with no Ultimate is asserted **unchanged as an identical canvas call sequence**, not merely as an identical hash, because a hash would pass even if the drawing had changed completely.

### Translate, not reimplement

The reference's freeze lives inside its `step()` (`core.js:1442`, *"both fighters frozen, timer paused, purely presentation"*) and costs 90 Ticks of simulation. Its VFX are a stepped particle pool with its own RNG, and its flash is a full-screen overlay whose alpha decays as `flashFrames / flashTotal`. None of the three survives here: a stepped pool cannot be scrubbed backwards, an ungoverned RNG contradicts the claim that a replay reproduces, and `docs/DESIGN.md` bans translucency outright (`style-discipline.test.ts` enforces it).

What was taken was the **behavioural facts** — 90 frames of held picture, a flash on the connect, a shake, an impact mark — expressed in this project's terms:

- **Freeze**: 90 extra clock frames. The reference's own count, re-homed. Its loop and this player's clock both advance one frame per animation-frame callback, so 90 there and 90 here are the same length of picture; only the owner differs.
- **Flash**: one solid `--tb-ink` plate over three frames. Deliberately not a strobe — an alternating plate is 30Hz here, which is a photosensitivity trigger, and one 50ms plate reads as the same impact while repeating nothing.
- **Impact mark**: a hard `--tb-accent` band struck from the caster toward whoever it landed on, with a heavier cap at the leading edge, expanding over 22 frames and then holding. Absent entirely on a whiff.
- **Streaks**: a phase-offset field rather than a particle pool. Each square owns a fixed slice of the cycle derived from its ordinal, so its position at any age is a pure function of `(filmIndex, agentIndex, ordinal, age)` through the same `scramble`/`jitter` the sparks already use. The field looks continuously alive and is still an array read.
- **Shake**: added *after* the hit shake is clamped and bounded by its own magnitude, rather than by widening `maxMagnitude`. Widening that cap would have let two ordinary simultaneous hits sum higher than they do today — a silent retune of every Match that has never seen an Ultimate.
- **Banner**: `ULTIMATE` in ground ink on a `--tb-warn` fill, the same warn-as-fill language 10.3 gave the armed gauge. Chosen so the cinematic reads as the payoff to `ULTIMATE READY` rather than as a separate idea.

Every duration and extent is a field in `DEFAULT_JUICE_TUNING.cinematic`. There is no literal at a call site.

### Reduced motion

The track keeps its shape — same frame count, same clock→film mapping, so the transport, the scrub and every index-based assertion behave identically. What goes is the three motions the preference exists to switch off: the full-stage luminance change, the moving streak field, and the camera shake. What is left is a still title card with the impact mark already at full reach. Asserted, and asserted non-vacuously (the same case checks the flash really was on without the preference).
