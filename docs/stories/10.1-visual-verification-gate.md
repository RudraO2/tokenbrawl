# Story 10.1 — The visual verification gate

As the developer, I want every story that changes what the page looks like to be verified against a rendered screenshot, so that visual defects stop shipping green.

**Goes first in Epic 10.** No other story in this epic lands before this one.

## Why this story exists

Three defects shipped in Epic 9 and survived in `main` until they were found by hand on 2026-08-06:

- Story 9.7 set `scale: 3` in the four roster sprite layouts. The packs' characters fill 158–175 of their 208 source pixels, so the drawn fighter was 474px tall in a 400px canvas — the two fighters overlapped and both ran off the top of the arena.
- Story 9.3's Spectate panel hardcoded the block artist and passed no backdrop, so it played as coloured rectangles on flat black while the replay player beside it ran the full art.
- Story 9.7 is titled "four-character custom roster". It shipped four sprite packs, wired two, and deferred character select to a story that was never written.

The suite was green for all three. It asserts canvas **call sequences**, and a wrong scale, a missing backdrop and an unwired asset all produce a perfectly valid call sequence. The test suite is structurally incapable of catching this class of defect.

The repository already knew this. `apps/web/src/render/artist.ts` carries the comment:

> *"Found by looking at the page during Story 4.3, not by any test."*

That observation was recorded and then not acted on. This story acts on it.

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** a story that touches rendering, sprites, layout, the HUD or VFX, **When** its exit gate runs, **Then** the gate includes starting the dev server, loading the page, and capturing a screenshot of the affected surface.

**Given** that screenshot, **When** the story is reviewed, **Then** it is compared against the corresponding reference shot in the NextGen reference project's `shots/` directory, and the comparison is recorded in the story file as a short written finding — not merely asserted to have happened.

**Given** the three rendering surfaces — the replay player (`#app`), Arcade (`#arcade`) and Spectate (`#spectate`) — **When** any story in this epic completes, **Then** all three are screenshotted, not only the surface the story named. The Spectate regression was invisible for precisely this reason: no story mentioned it, so no story looked at it.

**Given** a machine with no browser automation available, **When** the gate runs, **Then** it reports that the visual check was skipped and the story may **not** be marked done. A skipped check is never a pass.

**Given** `docs/stories/README.md`, **When** this story completes, **Then** the visual gate appears in the "Universal rules" section alongside `npm test` and `scripts/audit-invariants.sh`, so it binds every future story rather than only Epic 10's.

## Completion note

Shipped 2026-08-06.

- `docs/stories/README.md` gains the visual gate as a **universal rule**, beside `npm test` and `audit-invariants.sh`, so it binds every future story rather than only Epic 10's. It states the three-surface requirement, the reason it exists, and that a skipped check is never a pass.
- `docs/VISUAL-CHECK.md` is the runbook: the three surfaces and their host elements, the capture steps, and — the part worth having — a "what to actually look at" list derived from the three defects that got through, not from a generic checklist. It records the specific traps: sprite scale must be re-derived per pack from the character's own pixel height because the roster packs crop tight (158–175 of 208 source pixels); `anchorY` puts feet on the floor; `drawFrame` falls back from a missing artist index to index 0, so a half-filled artists array silently dresses both fighters identically.
- Step 2 of the runbook records a non-obvious trap found while writing it: sprites and the backdrop are deliberately off the critical path in `startup.ts` and swap into an already-running fight, so a screenshot taken immediately after load correctly shows the block artist and proves nothing. Wait ~2s.
- Step 4 records another: the asset loaders warn rather than throw, so a pack that failed to decode reports itself **only** in the console — on screen the block-artist fallback looks deliberate.

Two defects this gate was written for were found and fixed before it existed, which is the evidence it works: the `scale: 3` sprite overflow (all four roster layouts, now `scale: 1` with the derivation recorded in each `_comment`) and Spectate's missing art (`spectate/panel.ts` now carries a `dressing` box fed by `startup.ts`'s `dressArtist`/`dressBackdrop`).

No production code, no test code, as scoped.
