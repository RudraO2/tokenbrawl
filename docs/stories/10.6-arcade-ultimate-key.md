# Story 10.6 — A human can throw the Ultimate

As a player in Arcade Mode, I want a key that fires the Ultimate when my gauge is full, so that I can actually use the mechanic this epic builds.

## The key, and why it is a single key

The reference fires its super on `L`, but reaches it as a three-button chord — Light + Heavy + Blast pressed together at full meter. Tokenbrawl has no chord concept anywhere in its input handling, and introducing one for a single move buys nothing: there are no other chords for it to be consistent with, and a partially-landed chord is a source of exactly the "why didn't my Ultimate come out" complaint the reference's own comments record fighting.

**Decision: a single dedicated key, `L`.** It matches the reference's own key for the same move, so anyone moving between the two projects has one thing less to relearn.

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** Arcade Mode with the gauge full, **When** the player presses `L`, **Then** the Ultimate fires — alongside the existing on-screen `SPECIAL` button, which stays for touch.

**Given** the gauge below full, **When** the player presses `L`, **Then** nothing fires **and the panel says why**. A silent no-op is indistinguishable from a broken key, which is the same failure mode Story 9.3's picker guard exists to prevent.

**Given** the gauge reaching full during a Match, **When** it arms, **Then** the Arcade panel surfaces the affordance, so a player who never read the controls still discovers the mechanic. Story 10.3 arms the gauge on the canvas; this criterion is about the panel's own controls.

**Given** the existing bindings, **When** `L` is added, **Then** it collides with nothing — the arrow keys, `Z`, `X` and `C` all keep their current meaning.

**Given** the panel's help text, **When** it lists controls, **Then** `L` appears in it. A binding nobody is told about is not a feature.

**Given** an Arcade Match, **When** it completes, **Then** it is still excluded from rating (AD-14) — this story adds an input path, not a ranked mode.

### Story 10.4's warning was right about the risk and wrong about the cause

10.4's visual check measured peak Super Gauge fills of **62%, 55%, 1% and 1%**
across four hand-played Arcade Matches and concluded a human cannot bank 100
meter inside 40 Decision Points, warning that this story "must not assume the
gauge will be armed". Taken seriously, and it turned out to be a **range**
problem rather than a meter-rate one.

The fighters start 320 units apart. An attack thrown from there reaches nobody,
and a policy of pure attacking runs a whole Match to the tick limit at
**100/100 health and 0/0 meter on both sides** — measured, not guessed. That is
what the two 1% runs were: forty Decision Points of swinging at empty stage.
10.4's own note called them "total whiffs" without drawing the conclusion.

A policy that closes the distance banks a full bar comfortably. Advancing twice
for every attack, one press per Decision Point, reaches **peak meter 100 and
logs a `special`** on both seed 9201 (the panel's own default) and seed 4601.
Most of the bar comes from `meterOnHitTaken: 6` rather than from landing hits —
walking into a fight charges the gauge, which is a reasonable thing for the
mechanic to reward and required no balance change to discover.

**So no `packages/env-fighter` change was needed, and none was made.** The
gauge was always reachable; nobody had played toward it.

### The panel can now see the gauge, because nothing else can

`run.ts` gained `onLegalActions`, a read-only tee on the human Agent's
`observe`. The panel needs it for one reason: an Arcade Match runs **headlessly**
— `startup.ts` re-mounts the player only once the Match has finished — so Story
10.3's on-canvas Super Gauge is not on screen while anyone is actually playing.
Without this a player has no way to know their bar is full, and AC3 would have
nothing to surface.

`legalActions` is the environment's own answer (`legalActionsFor` returns every
Action once `meter >= specialMeterCost` and drops `special` below it), so "does
this list contain `special`" *is* "is the gauge full", read from the simulation
rather than recomputed beside it.

**The clamp is still in exactly one place.** `arcade/agent.ts`'s docblock is
emphatic that `createHumanAgent` is the only thing that turns an input into an
Action or drops it (AD-14), and that is unchanged: every press is still
forwarded unconditionally, including an Ultimate that cannot come out. What the
panel gained is the ability to *narrate* the drop, never to cause it — a test
asserts the press still reaches `feedInput` when the bar is empty, which is the
case that would fail if a second copy of the rule had crept into the UI. A
listener that throws is contained, so a UI callback cannot fail a Decision Point.

### `L` is an addition, not a rebind

`C` has meant `special` since Story 9.2 and still does; `L` joins it. Silently
moving the binding would break the muscle memory of anyone who has played, and
AC4 asks for exactly the opposite. Both keys get identical treatment, including
the AC2 explanation. A single key rather than the reference's three-button
chord, for the reason the story itself gives.

The on-screen `SPECIAL` button stays for touch and routes through the same
function the keyboard does, so a player on a phone gets the same sentence a
player on a laptop gets rather than silence.

**Three observations, none blocking, none fixed here:**

1. **10.2's edge-clipping defect reproduced again**, and this time on the frame
   that matters: the human threw the Ultimate with their back to the right wall
   and part of the fighter is drawn off-canvas in the cinematic capture. Still
   10.2's, still logged there, but it is now visible on the epic's showcase
   moment rather than only during ordinary movement — worth raising in priority.
2. **The player is blind during an Arcade Match.** The canvas only appears once
   the Match is over, so health, position and distance are invisible while
   playing; this story's affordance fixes that for the gauge alone. It is also
   why "attacking from the start position hits nobody" went unnoticed for two
   stories. A live Arcade canvas is its own story and would subsume 10.4's
   observation #1 about Spectate having no juice layer.
3. **Pressing only `L` for a whole Match never terminates.** The Harness blocks
   on the human Agent by design (INV-1) and an illegal input is dropped, so a
   player who presses nothing else waits forever. Correct per AD-14 and not
   introduced here, but with `L` now on the keyboard it is newly easy to reach;
   the AC2 sentence is what tells them why nothing is happening.
