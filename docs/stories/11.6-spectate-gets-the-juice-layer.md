# Story 11.6 — The stream everyone watches gets the effects

As a visitor, I want the always-running AI-vs-AI stream to have the same impact
effects and sound as the replay player, so that the surface I look at first and
longest is not the flattest thing on the page.

## Reference material

**Read `docs/DEV-REFERENCE.md` first** for the reference project's location and
the four mechanisms that may never be copied.

This story has **no reference counterpart** — the reference has no ambient
spectate stream. Everything here is wiring this repo's own existing juice and
audio layers into a surface that never got them, so nothing is copied and
nothing new is designed. If this story starts inventing a look, it has gone
wrong: it should be reusing `drawJuicedFrame` and `buildAudioTrack` verbatim.

## The gap, measured

`spectate/panel.ts` calls `drawFrame` **directly** — never `drawJuicedFrame` —
and `spectate/walk.ts` builds its clock from `film.frames.length` rather than
from a juice track. No audio track is built at all.

So Spectate has, and has always had: **no hitstop, no sparks, no damage numbers,
no cinematic, no sound.** Story 10.4's visual check confirmed it by watching 541
rendered frames covering the whole of `spectate-03` — the one streamed Match
that contains an Ultimate — and counting **zero** banner frames and **zero**
accent pixels below the HUD.

This is a Story 9.5-era gap that has been logged three times (10.4 observation
#1, 10.5 observation #1, 10.6) and fixed never. It is the largest single
presentation deficit in the project, because Spectate is the ambient surface: an
Ultimate on the replay player fires once when a visitor presses a button, and
Spectate runs forever.

## The thing that makes this non-trivial

Wiring the juice layer **lengthens every Spectate Match's clock**. A juice track
is the film's length plus every hitstop hold plus every cinematic freeze —
`spectate-03` is 480 film frames and 630 clock frames.

`manifest.json` records a `frameCount` per entry, and
`spectate/manifest.ts`'s `offsetForNow` uses it to compute where a visitor
arriving right now should join the loop. **That recorded number is currently the
film's length, not the track's.** Left alone, every entry's join offset drifts by
up to a third of a Match and the loop's timing goes wrong.

This is the real work of the story and it must be solved deliberately, not
discovered.

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** the Spectate stream, **When** a hit lands, **Then** it draws the same
impact art, hitstop and damage numbers the replay player draws, through the
**existing** juice layer — extended, never reimplemented alongside it.

**Given** an Ultimate in a streamed Match, **When** it plays, **Then** the
cinematic plays there too.

**Given** the juice layer lengthening the clock, **When** `manifest.json` is
regenerated, **Then** each entry's `frameCount` is the **track's** length, and
`offsetForNow` lands a visitor at the same point in the loop it always did.
Regeneration is by the committed script, not by hand.

**Given** the stream, **When** it plays, **Then** its audio follows the page's
existing three-bus layer and its mute/unlock behaviour is unsurprising — an
ambient surface that starts making noise unprompted is a defect, so the default
must be deliberate and recorded in the story.

**Given** a visitor's browser with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, **When**
Spectate draws, **Then** it honours the preference exactly as the player does.

**Given** any streamed Match, **When** its Final-State Hash is compared before
and after, **Then** it is unchanged (AD-15), and every committed
`spectate-0N.command-log.json` still verifies.

## Invariants at risk

- **AD-15** — presentation only; the Command Logs are not regenerated, only the
  manifest's derived frame counts.
- **AD-17** — the default stream stays a manifest walk over already-committed
  logs. Nothing here may turn it into a computation triggered by a visitor.
- **INV-3** — `loopStartEpochMs` is a fixed anchor constant, not a timestamp
  taken at generation time. It must stay one.

## Test plan

- Spectate's call sequence contains the juice layer's calls where the replay
  player's does.
- A streamed Match containing an Ultimate draws the cinematic.
- `offsetForNow` lands at the same *proportion* through a Match before and after
  the frame-count change.
- Every committed Command Log still verifies its hash.
- Reduced motion produces a still stream.

## Visual gate (Story 10.1) — run 2026-08-08

The capture this story exists for is the one the exit gate calls out: Spectate
mid-hit and mid-Ultimate, *"precisely the capture that has come back empty every
time it has been taken so far."* It came back full.

**How the state was reached.** `npm run dev` on **port 5175** (5173 and 5174
were both taken). Headless Chrome reports `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, and
after this story that is no longer a cosmetic difference — the stream is
deliberately a still frame under the preference — so `prefersReducedMotion` in
`main.ts` was temporarily stubbed to `false` for the visual claim and reverted
before the commit; `git status` is clean of it. `requestAnimationFrame` was
wrapped so the stream could be frozen on a chosen frame, and the canvas context
was instrumented to count paints (`drawJuicedFrame` and the `drawFrame` inside
it each clear the viewport, so a paint is two clears) and to record which
frames drew impact art, damage numbers, the `ULTIMATE` banner and the caster's
name. `DEMO_REPLAY_URL` was **not** repointed: Spectate plays the streamed logs
already, which is the whole point of the story.

**Spectate, mid-Ultimate.** `spectate-03` picked from the panel's own picker.
The cinematic opened **412 paints after the click**, of which ~11 belong to the
outgoing entry still drawing while the picked log was in flight — so it opens on
the entry's own clock frame ≈401, which is exactly where the track puts it. It
then drew on **81 contiguous paints**: **1** with Story 10.4's `ULTIMATE` banner
(the opening frame, before the portrait has eased in) and **80** with
CLAWDE's portrait and name, 160 `fillText` calls at two per frame for the
shadow-and-fill pair. The screenshot shows the letterbox plate, the portrait,
the name in the caster's aura, the gauge reading `ULTIMATE READY`, and particles
in the air. **The caster's name is the load-bearing detail**: it can only be
drawn when both the Ultimate sheet and the roster reached the paint, which is
the first-level degrade rather than the third this surface used to take by
default.

**Spectate, mid-hit.** Frozen one paint after the first impact-sheet draw: the
starburst is on the point of contact, the struck fighter is inside the red
hit-flash frame, sparks are in the air and the health bar's white lag layer is
trailing the damage. Across one measured run of `spectate-03`, **264 paints
carried a floating damage number**, the first of them 72 paints in. Before this
story every one of those counts was zero.

**The audio, measured on the real graph.** On a fresh load, with the stream
running and nothing clicked: **0 sources started and 0 gain writes**, for the
whole session. That is the recorded default — an ambient surface that starts
making noise unprompted is a defect, so Spectate's sound is off until asked for.
Pressing **`SOUND: OFF` → `SOUND: ON`** (`aria-pressed` flips with it) then gave,
over three seconds: `AudioContext.state` **`running`**, **549 gain writes —
183 per bus**, one `setGains` per painted frame in the order music, SFX, voice;
music held at **0.80**, SFX and voice at **1.0** (no duck, correct — the entry
playing carried no Ultimate and no KO); and **6 sources started: 1 looping** —
the music bed, started by the enable's own frame-0 presentation so that enabling
mid-Match sounds like enabling at the start of one — **and 5 one-shots** as the
bots traded. The network log carries `music_battle.mp3`, `sfx_hit_l.mp3`, and
then `sfx_ko.mp3` **and** `vo_ko.mp3` together: all three buses, live, on the
surface that had none of them.

**The mid-loop join, incidentally confirmed.** On one load the walk fetched
`spectate-04` first and worked forward through 05, 06, 01, 02 — a visitor
arriving mid-loop is dropped into the Match the loop is actually on (AD-17),
now computed against frame counts that describe the clock rather than the film.

**Arcade and the replay player**, screenshotted as the epic requires even though
this story names neither: both unbroken. The player draws its own juice layer
with the reasoning panel intact; Arcade renders Play vs CPU and all five
controls, Special included.

**Console**, filtered to error and warn: **none of either**. The only entries on
the page are Vite's dev-connection debug lines and Chrome's own verbose notice
about the BYOK key inputs, which predates this story. The loaders warn rather
than throw, so the absence of an "Impact FX unavailable" or "Ultimate FX
unavailable" line is the evidence that both sheets decoded and reached this
surface.

**A second gate pass, run on the owner's question, found the one real defect.**
Asked whether Spectate shows the actual character sprites, the page was
re-checked *without* the reduced-motion stub — the state a visitor with the
preference set actually gets — and the stream sat frozen on a still frame drawn
with the block artists on a black stage: no sprites, no backdrop. The cause is
this story's own. A reduced-motion clock paints once and never schedules again,
while `setArtist`/`setBackdrop`/`setVfx`/`setUlt` recorded the dressing and left
the redraw to "the walk's next frame". Under the preference there is no next
frame, and the packs land ~270ms after the first paint
(`/sprites/clawde/idle.png` responseEnd **273ms**, against a **144ms**
DOMContentLoaded), so they never arrived on screen at all. Every setter now
repaints, exactly as `mountPlayer`'s always have. Re-verified under the real
preference with no stub: the still frame draws the mountain-dusk backdrop,
Clawde and Chatty, and the arcade HUD.

**Nothing else found by the gate.** The other defect this story surfaced came earlier,
from the suite rather than the browser: passing the reduced-motion preference
into the walk turned a latent livelock live — a reduced-motion clock emits its
last frame immediately, which the walk read as "entry finished", so it would
have fetched the whole manifest in an unbounded microtask chain. Fixed before
the gate ran; the ambient loop no longer advances under the preference.
